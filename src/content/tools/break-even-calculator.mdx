  - question: What's the break-even formula?
    answer: "The break-even point in units is calculated as: Fixed Costs / (Price per Unit − Variable Cost per Unit). The denominator (Price − Variable Cost) is called the contribution margin — it's how much each sale contributes to covering fixed costs. For example, if your fixed costs are $50,000, your selling price is $100, and your variable cost per unit is $40, your break-even point is $50,000 / ($100 − $40) = 834 units. You need to sell 834 units before you start making a profit."
  - question: How do fixed and variable costs affect break-even point?
    answer: "Fixed costs (rent, salaries, insurance) raise your break-even point because they must be covered regardless of sales volume. Variable costs (materials, labor per unit) affect your contribution margin — higher variable costs mean less profit per sale, pushing your break-even point higher. Lowering either type of cost reduces your break-even point. Ideally, a business wants a high contribution margin (low variable costs) and manageable fixed costs for a low break-even point."
  - question: Why does break-even analysis matter for a new business?
    answer: "Break-even analysis tells you the minimum sales volume needed to avoid losing money — essential information for pricing, budgeting, and fundraising. It helps you answer critical questions: Is my business model viable? How many customers do I need? Can I realistically achieve that volume? Investors and lenders almost always ask for break-even projections. It also helps you understand how changes in costs or pricing impact profitability."
  - question: What is contribution margin?
    answer: "Contribution margin is the selling price per unit minus the variable cost per unit. It represents how much each unit sold contributes to covering fixed costs and generating profit. A product with a $50 selling price and $30 in variable costs has a $20 contribution margin. The contribution margin ratio (contribution margin / selling price) shows what percentage of each revenue dollar is available for fixed costs and profit. Higher contribution margins mean you break even faster."

A **break even calculator** is an essential tool for entrepreneurs, business owners, and managers evaluating the viability of a product or service. Knowing how many units you need to sell — or how much revenue you need to generate — before turning a profit is fundamental to business planning, pricing strategy, and financial forecasting.

## How to Use This Calculator

Enter your total fixed costs (rent, salaries, equipment, insurance — costs that don't change with production volume), your selling price per unit, and your variable cost per unit (materials, direct labor, shipping). The calculator will show your break-even point in units and in revenue dollars, plus your contribution margin and contribution margin ratio. Adjust any input to see how changes affect your break-even point.

## How It's Calculated

**Break-even Point (units) = Fixed Costs / (Price per Unit − Variable Cost per Unit)**

**Break-even Point (revenue $) = Break-even Units × Price per Unit**

**Contribution Margin per Unit = Price per Unit − Variable Cost per Unit**

**Contribution Margin Ratio = Contribution Margin per Unit / Price per Unit**

The break-even point is the minimum sales volume required to avoid a loss. Every unit sold beyond the break-even point generates profit equal to the contribution margin.
