  - question: How much house can I afford based on my salary?
    answer: "Most lenders follow the 28/36 rule: your monthly housing payment should not exceed 28% of your gross monthly income, and your total debt payments (including the new housing payment) should not exceed 36%. On an $80,000 annual salary ($6,667/month), the front-end limit is $1,867/month and the back-end limit is $2,400/month. After accounting for existing debts (say $500/month), your max housing payment is $1,867. At 6.5% interest, that supports about a $290,000 loan — plus your down payment determines the max home price."
  - question: What's the 28/36 rule?
    answer: The 28/36 rule is a widely used mortgage lending guideline. The "28" means no more than 28% of your gross monthly income should go toward housing costs (principal, interest, taxes, insurance, PMI, and HOA). The "36" means no more than 36% should go toward total debt (housing plus all other debt payments like car loans, student loans, and credit cards). Some lenders allow higher ratios — up to 43% or even 50% — with strong compensating factors like excellent credit.
  - question: How does down payment affect affordability?
    answer: Your down payment directly affects how much house you can afford in two ways. First, a larger down payment means a smaller loan, which lowers your monthly payment. Second, a down payment of at least 20% eliminates PMI, reducing your monthly cost by $100–$300 per month. For a $400,000 home, a 10% down payment ($40,000) results in a higher monthly payment than a 20% down payment ($80,000) due to both the larger loan and PMI costs.
  - question: What other costs should I consider besides the mortgage?
    answer: Homeownership costs go far beyond the mortgage payment. You'll need to budget for property taxes (0.5–2.5% of home value annually), homeowners insurance ($1,000–$2,000/year), PMI if down payment is under 20%, maintenance and repairs (experts recommend 1% of home value per year), HOA fees ($100–$500+/month), utilities, and closing costs (2–5% of purchase price). This calculator includes all of these to give you a true affordability picture.

A **home affordability calculator** helps you set a realistic budget before you start house hunting. It's exciting to browse homes online, but knowing what you can truly afford — including property taxes, insurance, PMI, and HOA fees — prevents the heartbreak of falling in love with a home that's out of reach. This calculator uses the standard 28/36 lending rule to give you a responsible, lender-approved budget.

## How to Use This Calculator

Enter your gross annual income, monthly debt payments (car loans, student loans, credit card minimums), and your down payment amount. Choose your expected interest rate and loan term. Add estimates for annual property taxes, homeowners insurance, and any HOA fees. The calculator will show your maximum affordable home price, the estimated monthly payment, and how the 28/36 rule applies to your situation.

## How It's Calculated

First, the lending limits are determined using the 28/36 rule:

**Max Housing Payment (front-end) = Gross Monthly Income × 0.28**

**Max Total Debt Payment (back-end) = Gross Monthly Income × 0.36**

**Max Housing Payment = min(front-end limit, back-end limit − other monthly debts)**

Then the amortization formula is solved in reverse for P (max loan amount) given M = Max Housing Payment minus estimated taxes, insurance, and PMI:

**P = M × [ (1+i)^N − 1 ] / [ i(1+i)^N ]**

**Max Home Price = P + Down Payment**

This gives you the maximum home price you can afford based on standard lending guidelines.
