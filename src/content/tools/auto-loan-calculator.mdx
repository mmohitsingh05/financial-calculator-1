    answer: A longer loan term — such as 72 or 84 months — lowers your monthly payment but costs significantly more in total interest. An **auto loan calculator** is the smartest way to understand what a car will really cost before you step onto the dealership lot. Beyond the sticker price, your monthly payment depends on your down payment, trade-in value, interest rate, loan term, and even sales tax. This calculator factors all of these in to give you a complete picture of your auto financing costs.

## How to Use This Calculator

Start with the vehicle price — this is the negotiated price or MSRP. Subtract your down payment and any trade-in value to find the amount you need to finance. Add any sales tax and dealer fees that will be rolled into the loan. Enter the interest rate (APR) and choose your loan term, typically 36 to 84 months. The calculator will instantly show your monthly payment, total interest paid, and the total cost of the vehicle including financing.

## How It's Calculated

Auto loans use the same standard amortizing formula as mortgages:

**M = P × [ i(1+i)^N ] / [ (1+i)^N − 1 ]**

Where **P** is the amount financed (vehicle price − down payment − trade-in + sales tax and fees included in the loan), **i** is the monthly interest rate (APR ÷ 12), and **N** is the total number of monthly payments (loan term in months).

Total interest = (M × N) − P. A full amortization schedule shows how much of each payment goes toward principal versus interest, with interest costs highest in the early months of the loan.
