  - question: How much should I contribute to my 401(k)?
    answer: A good starting point is to contribute at least enough to get your full employer match — that's free money. Beyond that, a common target is 10–15% of your pre-tax income including the match. In 2026, the IRS contribution limit is $23,500 ($31,000 including catch-up for those 50+).   - question: What is an employer match and how does it work?
    answer: An employer match is free money your company adds to your 401(k) based on your own contributions. A common structure is "100% match on the first 3% of salary, then 50% match on the next 2%." If you earn $60,000 and contribute 5% ($3,000), your employer adds $2,400 (100% of 3% = $1,800 + 50% of 2% = $600). That's an immediate 80% return on your contribution. Always contribute enough to capture the full match — it's the best guaranteed return you'll ever get.

The total contribution limit (including employer contributions) is $70,000 or $77,500 with catch-up. These limits are adjusted periodically for inflation. Note that your specific plan may have additional restrictions based on your salary and plan design.

A **401k calculator** is one of the most important retirement planning tools available. Your 401(k) is likely your largest retirement savings vehicle, and understanding how your contributions, employer match, and investment growth work together is essential. This calculator projects your future 401(k) balance and shows you the dramatic impact of employer matching and consistent contributions over time.

## How to Use This Calculator

Enter your current age, annual salary, current 401(k) balance, and your contribution percentage. Then input your employer's matching formula — typically something like "100% match up to 3% of salary, then 50% on the next 2%." Enter your expected annual return rate (7–10% is reasonable for a stock-heavy portfolio) and your retirement age. The calculator will show your projected balance at retirement, total personal contributions, total employer contributions, and total investment earnings.

## How It's Calculated

The 401(k) projection uses the same future value formula as other investment calculators:

**FV = Current_Balance × (1 + r/n)^(n×t) + Total_Contribution_Per_Period × [ ((1 + r/n)^(n×t) − 1) / (r/n) ]**

Total Contribution Per Period = Employee Contribution + Employer Match. The employer match is calculated based on the plan's formula — for example, 100% match on the first 3% of salary, plus 50% on the next 2%. Annual IRS contribution limits are applied as a cap. The calculator also applies annual salary increase and contribution limit adjustments for realistic long-term projections.
