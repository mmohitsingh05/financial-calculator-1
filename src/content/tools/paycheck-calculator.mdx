  - question: What's the difference between gross and net pay?
    answer: Gross pay is your total earnings before any deductions — your salary or hourly wages multiplied by the number of hours worked. Net pay (or take-home pay) is what actually lands in your bank account after all deductions. The difference includes federal income tax, Social Security and Medicare taxes (FICA), state income tax, and voluntary deductions like health insurance premiums, 401(k) contributions, and flexible spending accounts.
  - question: What deductions come out of my paycheck?
    answer: Mandatory deductions include federal income tax (withheld based on your W-4), Social Security tax (6.2% up to the annual wage base), Medicare tax (1.45%, plus an additional 0.9% for high earners), and state income tax (varies by state — some states have no income tax). Voluntary deductions may include health/dental/vision insurance, 401(k) or other retirement plan contributions, health savings account (HSA) or flexible spending account (FSA) contributions, and life insurance.
  - question: How do I estimate my take-home pay by state?
    answer: State income tax varies widely. Eight states have no income tax (Alaska, Florida, Nevada, South Dakota, Tennessee, Texas, Washington, Wyoming). New Hampshire taxes only interest and dividends. Other states use either flat tax rates or progressive brackets. For example, California's top rate exceeds 13%, while states like Pennsylvania use a flat 3.07%. Choose your state in this calculator to get an accurate estimate of your net pay.
  - question: How does my W-4 affect my paycheck?
    answer: Your W-4 form tells your employer how much federal income tax to withhold from each paycheck. The form accounts for your filing status, number of jobs, dependents, other income, and tax credits. If too little is withheld, you'll owe taxes at filing time; too much, and you'll get a refund. The IRS Tax Withholding Estimator can help you optimize your W-4. This calculator estimates withholding based on standard W-4 settings.

A **paycheck calculator** takes the mystery out of your take-home pay. Understanding the gap between your gross salary and what actually hits your bank account is essential for budgeting, job offer comparisons, and financial planning. This calculator accounts for federal income tax, FICA (Social Security and Medicare), state income tax, and common pre-tax deductions to give you an accurate net pay estimate.

## How to Use This Calculator

Enter your gross annual salary or hourly wage, pay frequency (weekly, biweekly, semi-monthly, or monthly), filing status, and your state. Add any pre-tax deductions like health insurance premiums or 401(k) contributions. The calculator will show your gross pay per period, total deductions per period, and your net take-home pay. You'll see a detailed breakdown of where every dollar goes.

## How It's Calculated

Gross Pay per period = Annual Salary / pay periods per year

Federal Income Tax is calculated using the progressive bracket formula applied pro-rata per period. FICA taxes:
- Social Security = Gross Pay × 6.2% (up to the annual wage base limit)
- Medicare = Gross Pay × 1.45% (+ 0.9% Additional Medicare Tax on wages above threshold)

State Income Tax = Gross Pay × state rate (varies by state; several states = 0%)

Net Pay = Gross Pay − Federal Tax − FICA (SS + Medicare) − State Tax − Other Deductions (401k, health insurance, etc.)
