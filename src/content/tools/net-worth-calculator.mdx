  - question: What counts as an asset vs. a liability?
    answer: Assets are anything you own that has monetary value — cash, bank accounts, investments, retirement accounts, real estate, vehicles, and valuable personal property. Liabilities are debts you owe — mortgage balance, auto loans, student loans, credit card balances, personal loans, and any other outstanding debt. Your net worth is simply total assets minus total liabilities. A positive net worth means you own more than you owe; a negative net worth means your debts exceed your assets.
  - question: What's a good net worth for my age?
    answer: Median net worth varies significantly by age. According to Federal Reserve data, the median net worth for Americans under 35 is about $14,000; for ages 35-44, about $91,000; for 45-54, about $168,000; for 55-64, about $213,000; and for 65+, about $266,000. These figures vary widely by income, education, location, and homeownership status. Your personal net worth trajectory matters more than how you compare to others — the key is that it trends upward over time.
  - question: How often should I track my net worth?

Consistent tracking helps you see progress, identify problem areas, and stay motivated. A **net worth calculator** gives you the single most important snapshot of your financial health. Your net worth — total assets minus total liabilities — is the truest measure of your financial progress. Unlike tracking just your income or savings, net worth captures the complete picture: what you own, what you owe, and where you stand on your journey to financial independence.

## How to Use This Calculator

List all your assets: cash and bank accounts, investments, retirement accounts (401k, IRA), real estate value, vehicle values, and other valuable property. Then list all your liabilities: mortgage balance, auto loans, student loans, credit card balances, personal loans, and any other debts. The calculator will subtract your total liabilities from your total assets to show your net worth. You can save your results and track changes over time to see your financial progress.

## How It's Calculated

The calculation is straightforward but profoundly important:

**Net Worth = Total Assets − Total Liabilities**

Assets include: cash, checking and savings accounts, investment accounts, retirement accounts, real estate equity (current market value), vehicle value, and other property. Liabilities include: mortgage balance, auto loans, student loans, credit card balances, personal loans, and other debts. The result — positive or negative — gives you a clear starting point for financial planning and a benchmark to improve over time.
