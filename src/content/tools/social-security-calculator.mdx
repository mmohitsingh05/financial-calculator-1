faqs:
  - question: At what age should I claim Social Security?
    answer: The optimal age to claim Social Security depends on your health, financial needs, life expectancy, and other retirement income. Claiming at 62 gives you the earliest but smallest benefit (about 30% less than Full Retirement Age). Delaying to age 70 maximizes your benefit (about 32% more than FRA). If you expect to live past 80, delaying is usually mathematically better. If you need the income or have health concerns, claiming earlier may be the right choice.
  - question: How is my benefit amount calculated?
    answer: Social Security calculates your benefit using your 35 highest-earning years, adjusted for wage inflation. These are averaged to find your Average Indexed Monthly Earnings (AIME). Your AIME is then applied to a progressive formula with three "bend points" to determine your Primary Insurance Amount (PIA) at Full Retirement Age. This PIA is then adjusted based on whether you claim early (reduction) or late (delayed retirement credits). The exact calculation is complex — this tool provides a simplified estimate.
  - question: Does working while claiming reduce my benefits?
    answer: If you claim Social Security before your Full Retirement Age and continue working, your benefits may be temporarily reduced through the Retirement Earnings Test. In 2026, if you're under FRA for the full year, $1 in benefits is withheld for every $2 earned above $22,320. In the year you reach FRA, $1 is withheld for every $3 earned above $59,160. Once you reach FRA, there is no reduction regardless of how much you earn, and any withheld benefits are recalculated into higher payments.
  - question: What is my Full Retirement Age?
    answer: Your Full Retirement Age (FRA) depends on your birth year. For those born 1960 or later, FRA is 67. If you were born 1943–1954, it's 66. For those born between 1955 and 1959, FRA gradually increases from 66 and 2 months to 66 and 10 months. Claiming before FRA permanently reduces your benefit; claiming after FRA increases it through delayed retirement credits up to age 70.

A **Social Security calculator** helps you make one of the most important retirement decisions: when to claim your benefits. The difference between claiming at 62 versus 70 can mean hundreds of thousands of dollars over your lifetime. This estimator provides a simplified projection to help you understand how your claiming age affects your monthly benefit and lifetime income.

## How to Use This Calculator

Enter your birth year to determine your Full Retirement Age, your estimated benefit at FRA (you can find this on your Social Security statement at ssa.gov), and choose the age at which you plan to claim. The calculator will show your estimated monthly benefit at different claiming ages from 62 to 70, the total lifetime benefits you'd receive at different life expectancies, and a comparison to help you choose the optimal claiming strategy.

## How It's Calculated

This is a simplified educational estimator based on Social Security's adjustment factors:

**Benefit_at_claim_age = FRA_Benefit × adjustment_factor**

- Claiming before FRA: benefit is reduced by approximately 5/9 of 1% per month for the first 36 months early, plus 5/12 of 1% per month for additional months beyond 36.
- Claiming after FRA (up to age 70): benefit is increased by approximately 2/3 of 1% per month (8% per year) through delayed retirement credits.

Note: This is a simplified estimate. The exact SSA calculation uses 35-year AIME/PIA bend-point formulas requiring full earnings history. For your official benefit estimate, visit ssa.gov.
