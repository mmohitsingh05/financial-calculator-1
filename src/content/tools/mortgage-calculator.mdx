    answer: "Your monthly mortgage payment typically includes four components, often called PITI: Principal (the loan amount you're paying down), Interest (the cost of borrowing), Taxes (property taxes, usually held in escrow), and Insurance (homeowners insurance, plus PMI if your down payment is under 20%).   - question: How much house can I afford based on my income?
    answer: "Most lenders use the 28/36 rule: your monthly housing payment should not exceed 28% of your gross monthly income, and your total debt payments (including housing) should not exceed 36%. On a $100,000 annual salary ($8,333/month), that means a max housing payment of about $2,333/month. After accounting for taxes, insurance, and PMI (roughly $400–$800/month), the remaining amount for principal and interest determines your maximum loan amount and home price."
  - question: What's the difference between a 15-year and 30-year mortgage payment?
    answer: A 30-year mortgage offers lower monthly payments because the loan is stretched over twice as many years, but you pay significantly more total interest. A 15-year mortgage has higher monthly payments but you build equity much faster and pay far less interest over the life of the loan. For example, on a $300,000 loan at 6%, the 30-year payment is about $1,799/month while the 15-year payment is about $2,531/month — but the 30-year costs $347,514 in total interest versus $155,682 for the 15-year.

Buying a home is one of the biggest financial decisions you'll ever make, and a **mortgage calculator** is the essential first step to understanding what you can afford. This tool helps you estimate your monthly mortgage payment by factoring in not just the loan amount and interest rate, but also property taxes, homeowners insurance, PMI, and HOA fees — giving you a complete picture of your true monthly housing cost before you start house hunting.

## How to Use This Calculator

Start by entering the home price you're considering and your down payment amount. A 20% down payment eliminates the need for PMI, but many loans accept as little as 3–5% down. Next, input your interest rate — you can check current rates from major lenders or use a national average. Choose your loan term (typically 15 or 30 years) and add estimates for annual property taxes and homeowners insurance. If you know your HOA fees or expect to pay PMI, include those too. The calculator will instantly show your estimated monthly payment, the total interest paid over the life of the loan, and a full amortization schedule.

## How It's Calculated

This calculator uses the standard amortizing loan payment formula:

**M = P × [ i(1+i)^N ] / [ (1+i)^N − 1 ]**

Where **M** is your monthly payment, **P** is the loan amount (home price minus down payment), **i** is the monthly interest rate (your annual rate divided by 12), and **N** is the total number of monthly payments (loan term in years × 12).

The full monthly payment shown (PITI) adds property taxes, insurance, PMI, and HOA fees:
- Monthly property tax = (home value × annual tax rate) / 12
- Monthly PMI applies only if down payment < 20%, typically 0.5%–1.5% of loan amount annually

Total interest over the loan life is calculated as (M × N) − P, giving you the full cost of borrowing.
