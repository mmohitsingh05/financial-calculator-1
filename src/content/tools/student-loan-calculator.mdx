faqs:
  - question: How is student loan interest calculated?
    answer: Federal student loans use simple daily interest. Your daily interest is calculated as (current principal balance × interest rate) / 365.25. This accrued interest is capitalized (added to your principal) at certain events — like when you enter repayment after deferment or when you change repayment plans. Private loans may use different methods, so check your loan documents. Understanding how interest accumulates is key to minimizing what you pay over the life of your loan.
  - question: What's the difference between subsidized and unsubsidized loans?
    answer: Subsidized loans are need-based and the government pays the interest while you're in school at least half-time, during the six-month grace period, and during deferment. Unsubsidized loans are available regardless of financial need, and interest accrues from the day the loan is disbursed — even while you're in school. This means unsubsidized loans typically cost more over their lifetime because interest builds up before you ever start making payments.
  - question: How can I pay off student loans faster?
    answer: The most effective strategies include making extra payments toward the principal (even small amounts help), paying biweekly instead of monthly (one extra payment per year), applying windfalls like tax refunds or bonuses directly to the loan, enrolling in automatic payments (many lenders offer a 0.25% rate reduction), and refinancing to a lower rate if you have strong credit and stable income.

A **student loan calculator** is essential for understanding how your education debt will fit into your post-graduation budget. With the average student loan borrower carrying over $37,000 in debt, knowing your monthly payment under different repayment plans — from standard 10-year plans to income-driven options — helps you make informed decisions about repayment strategy, budgeting, and even career choices.

## How to Use This Calculator

Enter your total student loan balance, the average interest rate across your loans, and the repayment term you're considering. You can also enter expected monthly extra payments to see how much faster you can become debt-free. The calculator shows your standard monthly payment, total interest over the life of the loan, and how extra payments accelerate your payoff date. Compare standard, extended, and income-driven repayment scenarios side by side.

## How It's Calculated

The core formula is the standard amortization payment:

**M = P × [ i(1+i)^N ] / [ (1+i)^N − 1 ]**

For a standard 10-year plan, N = 120 monthly payments. For income-driven repayment (simplified estimate): monthly payment = (Discretionary Income × payment percentage) / 12, where Discretionary Income = AGI − (poverty line multiplier × federal poverty line). This calculator provides a simplified estimate; exact IDR formulas vary by plan (SAVE, PAYE, IBR). For capitalized interest scenarios, the new principal after deferment equals P plus accrued unpaid interest.
